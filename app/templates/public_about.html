About

News should just tell you what happened.

Instead, it tells you what happened and nudges you toward how to feel about it — through word choice, what gets left out, what gets the bigger headline. Not always on purpose. But it happens, a lot, because outlets run on ads, sponsors, and clicks, same as everything else online.

We can’t fix that part. What we can do is show you the framing, out loud, instead of leaving you to guess.

How it works

For every story, we pull coverage from multiple outlets and tag it: pro-establishment, anti-establishment, or apolitical — based on the government it’s actually about (Centre or a specific state, whoever’s in power right now). Not left vs. right. That’s a US framework and it doesn’t really map onto Indian politics.

If there’s an original source behind a story — an official statement, data, a speech — we link straight to it. That’s the Primary Source tag. Go check it yourself, don’t take our word for it either.

Who’s doing the tagging

First pass: an algorithm. Second pass: a real, trained journalist, reading the article blind — no outlet name attached, just the words — who either agrees or overrides it. Nothing goes live without a human checking it.

We keep our reviewers anonymous. Not for mystery points — because the second people know who’s tagging what, they get pressure, backlash, DMs. Anonymity is what lets them actually call it straight.

We’re not pretending to be neutral robots either

Deciding what counts as “establishment,” where a story lands, when a random accident story turns political — that’s judgment. Ours. We’re not going to pretend we’re above the thing we’re calling out in everyone else. What we can promise: we do it the same way every time, a human’s always in the loop, and we’ll tell you how it works instead of asking you to just trust the label.

Where we’re at

Early. Indian news, English language, a handful of outlets to start, more coming.
